1. Executive Summary

What Udupi Civic Watch is

Udupi Civic Watch is a mobile-first civic cleanliness reporting platform. Residents can report waste issues with a photo and GPS location. The report is assigned into a government-side workflow for allocation, cleanup, monitoring, and closure.

What problem it solves

Waste complaints are often difficult to report, assign, track, and verify. The platform creates a report number, records location and evidence, identifies the relevant service area, assigns responsibility, and lets officials update progress.

Who uses it

  • Citizens: submit and track reports without an account.
  • Field officers: view assigned reports, start cleanup, complete work, and upload completion photos.
  • Panchayat administrators and Control Center staff: monitor reports, officers, status, and local performance.
  • Hierarchy roles: Commissioner, Environmental Engineer, Health Inspectors, Supervisors, and Community Mobilisers have dedicated accounts or dashboards with varying scope.

Main purpose

To create an auditable, location-aware workflow from a resident’s waste complaint to government cleanup action.

What makes it useful

  • Combines photo evidence, GPS, ward/service-area checks, routing, status tracking, maps, and official dashboards.
  • Supports complaint and cleanup photos, live maps, browser push notifications, and conditional email notifications.
  • Provides public citizen services and official workflow tools in one system.

Overall conclusion: The platform has substantial implemented functionality, but it is not ready for an unrestricted public-government launch without security, abuse-prevention, operations, privacy, and recovery improvements.

2. Citizen Features

2.1 Submit a waste report — Implemented

Citizens can create a waste complaint without an account. The report screen requests GPS location, requires at least one photo, uploads evidence, checks the service area, and creates a report.

Collected: latitude/longitude, one or two complaint photos, optional description, optional reporter email, reporter IP for abuse controls, assignment, and timestamps.

Media: photos supported; no evidence of video or document uploads. Location is normally mandatory. New reports trigger official notifications where assignment/configuration permits; a reporter email triggers an attempted acknowledgement.

Tracking: yes. The success screen provides a report number and a direct tracking action.

2.2 Duplicate-report prevention — Implemented

The API checks for an active nearby report from the previous 24 hours and returns the existing report reference when a likely duplicate is found. This is a practical approximate geographic check, not a guarantee of perfect duplicate detection.

2.3 Service-area validation — Implemented

Configured polygon data is used by the client and API to reject locations outside the defined Saligrama or Udupi service zones. Public use is GPS-led; manual placement is primarily test-mode behavior.

2.4 Public report tracking — Implemented, simplified history

Citizens can enter a report number and see Reported, Cleaning, or Cleaned status. The page polls about every 10 seconds and displays complaint/cleanup photos in a lightbox. The visual timeline is derived from current timestamp fields; it is not a complete server-maintained event history.

2.5 My Reports — Implemented locally

The browser stores up to 20 recent report IDs and reloads their public status. This is device-local storage, not a citizen account or cross-device history.

2.6 Public live map — Implemented

The Leaflet/OpenStreetMap map displays status markers, ward/service overlays, report popups, and complaint/cleanup photos. It refreshes periodically and can show a one-time “You Are Here” dot. Continuous walking accuracy and a confirmed one-tap location control are not evidenced. Precise public coordinates, descriptions, addresses, and media require a privacy policy before launch.

2.7 Citizen browser push — Implemented, conditional

Citizens can subscribe to report-linked browser notifications for cleanup started and cleaned events. Delivery depends on browser permission, service-worker operation, VAPID configuration, and network availability.

3. Administration Features

Control Center dashboard

Provides report totals, status summaries, panchayat/ward/officer/date filtering, trends, zone/map views, and performance summaries.

Report management

  • Control Center can view, search, filter, inspect, reassign, archive, bulk archive, and manage archived reports.
  • Panchayat administrators can view panchayat-scoped reports, filter by status/ward/officer, update status, archive records, and inspect photos.
  • Restricted purge/delete capabilities exist for archived records, but retention, approval, reason, and immutable audit controls are not sufficiently evidenced.

Officer management

Control Center can create, edit, deactivate/delete, filter, and assign geographic area information to field officers. Welcome email is attempted if SMTP is configured.

Status management

Officials can move cases through Reported → Cleaning → Cleaned. Cleaning and completion timestamps, cleanup photos, email, in-app, and push notifications are linked to these transitions.

Analytics and reports

Dashboards provide status totals, resolution rates, waste-type patterns, ward/officer/panchayat views, response-time calculations, and conditional AI narrative summaries. Some computations may need database-side optimization as volume grows.

Search and filtering

Search/filtering exists across several official views for status, ward, officer, panchayat, waste type, and text. Reporter-email filtering, complete hierarchy-wide report-number search, and archive-reason filtering are not all confirmed.

Other administration

Role-specific dashboards exist for Commissioner, Environmental Engineer, Health Inspector, Supervisor, and Community Mobiliser. Their confirmed write capabilities vary; several appear primarily read/monitoring-oriented.

4. Department / Officer Workflow

  1. Citizen submits: photo, GPS, optional description/email.
  2. Validation: request shape, duplicate check, report rate limit, and service-area check.
  3. Assignment: coordinates are matched to an officer area; a report may remain unassigned if no match exists.
  4. Notification: Control Center is notified; assigned officer and relevant panchayat admin are notified where data exists; email is conditional.
  5. Processing: field officer views work, marks Cleaning, then Cleaned, and can upload cleanup photos.
  6. Citizen update: public tracking refreshes; email and browser push are conditional.
  7. Closure: Cleaned is the current resolution endpoint with completion timestamp and optional evidence.
  8. Archival: archiving/purge is a separate administrative function.

Difference from a full case-management workflow: no strong evidence of formal rejection, escalation, SLA breach, reopen, quality approval, dispute, or immutable action history.

7. Location / GIS Features

  • GPS capture and coordinate storage.
  • Polygon-based service-area validation.
  • Udupi/Saligrama ward and geofence data.
  • Leaflet/OpenStreetMap interactive maps.
  • Public and official report markers.
  • Area-based officer assignment and role scope.
  • Ward overlays and location-based filtering.

11. Security

Implemented controls

  • Bcrypt password hashes.
  • Role-specific API authorization middleware.
  • Geographic scope checks in multiple routes and tests.
  • Zod request validation.
  • Report rate limit and duplicate checks.
  • Service-area rejection.
  • Filename sanitization on media retrieval.
  • Request logging.
  • Soft deletion in selected flows.

Launch-critical weaknesses

Critical — default credentials and fallback secret: startup behavior can create/reset a known control-center credential, and authentication has a fallback secret if configuration is missing.
Critical — custom session signing: the session construction is custom rather than a standard reviewed authenticated-session mechanism.
Critical — public uploads: the upload route is public and lacks evidenced content inspection, robust allow-listing, pixel/dimension limits, upload quotas, scanning, orphan cleanup, and explicit privacy controls.
High — permissive credentialed CORS: production origins need strict allow-listing and CSRF review.
High — public privacy exposure: precise locations, descriptions, addresses, and media may be exposed through the public map.
High — limited abuse controls: login, tracking, upload, password reset, and other public endpoints do not show comprehensive centralized throttling.
High — operational gaps: health checks do not verify database/storage/push/scheduler readiness; no tested backup/restore, centralized error monitoring, or alerting was evidenced.

Security conclusion: The authorization foundation is promising, but the system should be treated as pre-production until the critical and high-risk findings are addressed and independently tested.

13. Current Limitations

  • Scalability: some analytics and map/list operations may become expensive as volume increases.
  • Security: default credential/session, upload, CORS, privacy, and rate-control risks require action.
  • Reliability: external notifications are often fire-and-forget; dependency health is lightly checked.
  • Data protection: no documented tested backup/restore, media lifecycle, or privacy/retention program was found.
  • Citizen experience: GPS dependence, basic report search, device-local history, and limited accessibility/local-language evidence.
  • Administration: limited escalation, approval, quality-review, reopening, and audit-history capabilities.
  • Infrastructure: no complete repository-defined production monitoring, rollback, or incident response path.

16. Project Complexity Assessment

Assessment: Advanced application, progressing toward a production-grade civic technology platform.

This is substantially more than a basic form or CRUD dashboard. It combines public reporting, GPS/geofencing, maps, multi-photo media, role-specific government workflows, assignment, status lifecycle, dashboards, analytics, PostgreSQL, object storage, email, push, scheduling, API generation, AI capability, and automated tests.

It should not yet be described as production-grade public civic infrastructure because security hardening, privacy, backup/recovery, monitoring, formal deployment governance, and end-to-end assurance remain incomplete.

18. Final Two-Page Summary

Udupi Civic Watch is a digital public-cleanliness reporting system designed to connect citizens with local government cleanup teams. Residents can report waste issues from a mobile device by taking a photo and allowing the application to capture their GPS location. The system gives the complaint a report number, checks whether the location falls within the service area, tries to identify the responsible field officer, and allows the citizen to follow progress.

The citizen-facing service is straightforward: report waste, attach photo evidence, receive a report number, and track the complaint as it moves from Reported to Cleaning to Cleaned. Citizens may optionally provide an email address for acknowledgements and status updates, and may subscribe to browser alerts for cleanup progress. A public map shows reported issues and their progress, while available complaint and cleanup photos can be viewed.

For government users, the platform provides operational dashboards and role-based access. Field officers can see assigned work, begin cleanup, mark work complete, and upload evidence. Panchayat administrators can monitor local reports, review officers and status, and archive records. The Control Center can view system-wide reports, manage field officers, filter and search data, reassign work, monitor analytics, and manage archived records. Dedicated dashboards also exist for Commissioner, Environmental Engineer, Health Inspector, Supervisor, and Community Mobiliser roles.

The platform includes geographic service boundaries, ward overlays, officer-area assignment, report maps, analytics by status and area, email notifications, browser push notifications, and scheduled weekly summaries. It also has a foundation for AI-assisted operational summaries and waste-photo analysis, though those features should be treated as optional and still maturing.

Technically, the platform is a modern web application built with React, Node.js, PostgreSQL, maps, cloud image storage, email, browser push messaging, and automated testing. It is more capable than a simple complaint portal because it combines citizen reporting, location-aware routing, media evidence, official workflows, notification tools, and analytics in one system.

The current application is best described as an advanced civic reporting platform suitable for pilot or controlled operational use after security improvements. It is not yet fully ready for an open public-government launch. The most urgent requirements are to remove unsafe default account/session behavior, harden official login and browser sessions, secure public uploads, control media/privacy exposure on the public map, introduce stronger rate limiting, add full monitoring and health checks, and establish tested backup/recovery procedures.

With these improvements, Udupi Civic Watch can become a strong operational platform for improving cleanliness reporting, accountability, transparency, and response coordination across Udupi’s wards and local bodies.
